# Intake — the question pool

Pick at most eight, by the priority in `SKILL.md`. Every question carries a recommended answer; the
recommendation is what a careful colleague would assume from the resume, and it is there so the user
can say "yes" in one word when it is right.

## 1 · Hard filters (ask first; almost never on a resume)

❓ **Work authorization** — "Are you authorized to work in {country} without sponsorship now and in
the future? (citizen / permanent resident / visa needing sponsorship / other)"
➡️ Recommend the status the resume implies (a long local work history → "citizen or permanent
resident"); ask because a wrong assumption wastes every application.

❓ **Location and on-site** — "Where are you based, which cities would you relocate to, and is
on-site / hybrid / remote a hard line for you?"
➡️ Recommend "current city, open to hybrid, remote preferred" unless the resume says otherwise;
this row becomes a hard filter in every assessment.

❓ **Clearance and licences** — "Do you hold an active security clearance, or any licence a role in
your field lists as required?"
➡️ Recommend "none" when nothing on the resume suggests one; roles that require an active clearance
are then filtered rather than attempted.

❓ **Degree** — "Highest completed degree and field — and is it what the resume shows?"
➡️ Recommend what the education section says; ask only if it is missing or ambiguous.

## 2 · Scale numbers missing from VERIFIED facts

❓ **The real number** — "f012 says you built the provisioning platform. How many machines did it
handle at peak, roughly, in a number you would say out loud?"
➡️ Recommend leaving it blank if the user is unsure. A blank scale is a fact you can still talk
about; an estimated one is the first thing an interviewer probes.

## 3 · Ownership vs participation

❓ **Who owned it** — "f020 says 'responsible for the migration'. Did you own the decision and the
work, or were you one of several people on it?"
➡️ Recommend `VERIFIED` only when the user owned the work; "part of the team" is `EXPOSURE` with the
statement rewritten to say what *you* did.

## 4 · The target

❓ **Title and direction** — "Which one or two job titles are you actually applying for, and which
direction is the lane (for example: Linux / platform, endpoint, network, AI infrastructure)?"
➡️ Recommend the title of the most recent role plus the one the resume's skills lean toward; this
is what the question bank and the tailoring key on.

## 5 · Employers and leaving

❓ **The legend** — "E1 = {name as written}, E2 = {…} — correct? And for each, the one-line reason
for leaving, in the words you have decided to use."
➡️ Recommend the neutral form ("the team was reorganised", "the contract ended", "I moved for the
role") and say that this line is repeated in every interview, so it is worth deciding once.

## 6 · Don't say

❓ **Never in the room** — "Two or three things you have decided never to say in an interview — a
way of describing a departure, an internal system name, a tool you have not run described as if you
had."
➡️ Recommend the three most common: no negative framing of a departure, no former employer's
internal names, no tool claimed beyond its tag. Written to `## Don't say`, read by `talk-track`.

## Not asked here

Anything about what the resume *should* say. Intake records what is true; shaping happens in
`jd-teardown` and `talk-track`, from these facts.
